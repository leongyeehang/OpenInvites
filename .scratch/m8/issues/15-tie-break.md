# 15: Voting: a tie for first place can go to a tie-break among the tied items

**What to build:** Once voting has closed, an award whose first place is shared offers its hosts a tie-break: a new round among the tied items only, a fresh vote each, counts hidden from guests. Closing it puts the tied items in order among themselves by that round's votes, in the places they shared, so two items that shared first become first and second; every other item keeps its place from the first round. If the top is still level, the host chooses another round among those still level, or keeps the shared first place. A tie-break is offered for first place only, award by award.

How it landed:
- **Ruling 1: the stored finalists are the rounds actually held.** `finalists` holds every tie-break round's finalists in turn, each round's list after the one before it: starting a round appends `tiedForFirst(awardPlaces(...))` (the ticket said "set"), never replacing the list. Another round is always among some of the last round's items, so `tiebreakRounds` parts the list where an item of the list before comes again; list *n* is round *n* + 2's. `finalPlaces` places every item by the last round it was held in and its votes there: the finalists of the deepest round first, in that round's order; those an earlier round placed after them, in that round's order (so a finalist who dropped out in round 2 keeps the place round 2 gave it, after round 3's finalists); every other item by its first-round votes, after all finalists. No tie is ever worked out again from first-round counts, so a reply deleted after a tie-break (its votes go by the cascade) cannot undo the order a held round gave. Unit cases: a finalist losing a first-round vote after a closed tie-break keeps the order; three rounds with a drop-out.
- **Ruling 2.** `awardPlaces` drops finalists that are no longer `placeable` (approved, in the award's scope), so places are those of the items still here; during a tie-break the award lists its round's finalists still here, and `tied` counts them. `closeTiebreak` needs nothing but an open tie-break, so one finalist left can be closed. A new rule follows from the list's shape: after a tie-break, another round is offered only when the items now level at the top are among the last round's finalists (`tiebreakOffered`), which fails only when every one of them has been deleted; then the shared first shows with no offer, and Reopen voting starts afresh. Unit cases for both.
- What is kept is which items each round was held among. The order within a round still follows its votes, so a tie-break vote deleted later (its voter's reply deleted) can change it, as any deleted vote changes a count; it cannot move an item into or out of a round.
- The hosts' box: while voting, Close voting; on the results and during a tie-break, Reopen voting (which ends a tie-break, ticket 14), and during a tie-break Close the tie-break before it (ticket 12's note: the box's button closed the event's vote during a tie-break, which did nothing). The offer is the text and Start a tie-break / Start another round with Keep the shared first place.
- A guest's page during a tie-break carries no `shownVotes` for that award, first round's included (`countsShown("tiebreak", guest)` is false), and no `tiebreakVotes` until it closes. The tile's count reads "2 votes, 2 votes in the tie-break" (the two messages joined with ", ", since no message joins them); the viewer shows "2 votes in the tie-break" beside the count (`data-slot="vote-tiebreak"`).
- The stars are unchanged: a new round's `voted` is the viewer's vote in that round, none at first. The browser test checks it on Ana's page drawn again by her comment, without a reload, after she voted in round 1.
