# 11: Design drawer: Sheet RSVP style and adaptive drawer

**What to build:** A host chooses whether guests answer inline under the buttons or in a frosted sheet that rises over the invitation, and the Design drawer itself becomes a bottom sheet on phones and a side panel on desktop.

**Blocked by:** 10 Design drawer: templates and knobs

**Status:** done

- [x] An RSVP style row in the Details group with Inline and Sheet; templates set it (Birthday uses Sheet, Festival uses Inline).
- [x] Sheet, from the prototype: the three status buttons stay under the poster; choosing one opens a frosted bottom sheet that grows through the steps while the invitation stays visible behind it; the confirmed sheet can be closed and reopened; focus is trapped inside the sheet and returns on close; the Design button moves out of the way while a sheet is open.
- [x] The drawer renders as a bottom sheet at phone widths and as a side panel at desktop widths, decided by viewport, with the page still visible and updating in both.
- [x] All motion stops under reduced motion.
- [x] Browser tests at 390px and desktop for both RSVP styles and for the drawer's two forms, including keyboard-only completion of the Sheet flow.

## Comments

**2026-09-23, implementing agent.** Done. Lint, type check, 185 unit tests, and 142 browser tests (71 scenarios at 390px and desktop) pass against the production image on the Compose `test` profile. The new browser specs also passed four repeats each. In dev mode the Sheet flow and the drawer log no console warnings or errors.

How it landed:

- **One flow, two containers.** `src/app/e/[slug]/rsvp-flow.tsx` has one set of steps, one form, one confirmation, and the same server actions and validation for both styles; only what they appear in differs. Inline: the steps open in a glass card under the buttons, and the confirmation takes the buttons' place (unchanged from ticket 07). Sheet: the three buttons stay under the poster; choosing one opens `RsvpSheet`, a Radix `Dialog` (modal) that rises from the foot of the screen, grows and shrinks with its step (its height follows its content through a ResizeObserver, with a CSS height transition), and holds the steps and then the confirmation. The confirmed sheet closes with a Close button or Escape; under the buttons, "You replied as Priya Nair. Show my RSVP" opens it again, and it is what a returning guest sees instead of a sheet in the way. Closing part-way leaves the RSVP as it was. Focus is trapped by Radix and goes back to the button that opened the sheet or, when that has gone (Change my answer, Remove my RSVP), to the status button being chosen from. A tap on the page behind does nothing, so tapping away the keyboard mid-answer keeps the guest's place. The sheet is portalled into the page's own element (`ThemedPage` now puts it in context as `root`), so it wears the theme.
- **The RSVP style row** is last in Details: Inline or Sheet, with a hint for the one chosen. `KnobChange` and `parseThemeChange` take `rsvpStyle`; `ResolvedTheme` carries it.
- **Template choices.** Birthday uses Sheet (round one's A) and Festival Inline (round one's B), as the ticket says. **Vows now uses Sheet** (the prototype had Inline): a wedding invitation is the thing guests keep, and answering on a card laid over it, like the reply card in a printed invitation, leaves it whole; its white accent and outline buttons read as a formal reply card on the frost. **Supper club stays Inline**: it was made for Broadsheet, whose one reply form sits in the page, and the editorial look keeps the answer in the flow of it. **Kids' party stays Inline**: it was made for Thread, where you answer in place in the conversation, and parents on the move get the steps right under the button they tapped. **Quiet stays Inline**: "Nothing moves", and a sheet rising over the page is both a layer and a motion.
- **The sheet is a legibility surface.** `legibility.ts` has `SHEET_SURFACES`: the sheet itself, fields and chips on it, the copy button on the edit link's pill, and the accent ink on it. Because the sheet rises over the page itself (cards, text, accent-filled buttons), what is behind it is unknown, so it is solved against the far end of the scale outright: white under light text, black under dark. It is tinted with the tone's shade as little as that allows (a plum frost at 67 to 73% in the light tone, a white one at 60% in the dark, close to the prototype's 70% plum and 80% white), and carries its own secondary strengths and accent ink (never closer than 80% to full strength), which it sets on itself through `sheetVariables` so the flow inside it keeps its classes and the page keeps its lighter values. Unit tests check every background, both tones, every accent: on the sheet's surfaces, and directly over white, black and the backdrop; that the sheet is never solid (at most 90%); that secondary text keeps its hierarchy; and that the page's own values are untouched. Two mutations (dropping the "anything behind" layer, solving on the bare sheet only) each fail the tests.
- **The drawer's two forms, in CSS alone,** at the `md` breakpoint (768px), already used by the shadcn components and by the prototype for exactly this switch (`sm` would leave the page 260px beside a side panel). Under it, a bottom sheet: edge to edge from the foot of the screen, at most 62dvh, and the page gets that much padding at its foot so all of it can scroll up above the sheet. From md up, a side panel down the right-hand side, and the page moves over beside it (`pr-101`). The page makes room through a `designing` custom variant (`body:has([data-slot="design-panel"])`); the panel is only rendered once opened, so server rendering cannot flash a wrong form. Resizing a desktop window to phone width with the drawer open turns it into the sheet (tested).
- **The Design button steps aside** to the top right while an RSVP sheet is open, through a second custom variant, `rsvp-sheet-open`.
- **Reduced motion:** the sheet's rise, fade and height transition, the drawer's slide, and the transitions on the flow's buttons and chips (and on the status buttons) are all `motion-safe` or switched off under `motion-reduce`. Browser tests emulate reduced motion and check that nothing runs (`getAnimations()` is empty, animation-name and transition are none), with a control that the same elements do animate when motion is welcome.
- **Browser tests:** `e2e/rsvp.spec.ts` is now explicitly the Inline style; `e2e/rsvp-sheet.spec.ts` is the Sheet (answer, grow, confirm, close, reopen, come back, change the answer; focus trap and return and the Design button; keyboard-only completion; reduced motion). `e2e/design.spec.ts` adds the RSVP style row with templates, the drawer's two forms with the page in view and changing, and the drawer under reduced motion. `createPublished` takes `rsvpStyle`, set in the Design drawer as a host would.

Decisions and things to know:

- **New events now get the Sheet**, because they start from Birthday. Eight scenarios in the older specs came back to the page expecting the confirmation in place of the buttons, which is the Inline behaviour; `rsvp.spec.ts` and those five other tests (cancel, calendar there and then, host changes a guest's answer, two question tests) now ask for Inline explicitly. Every other flow test passes unchanged with the flow inside the sheet. `decisions.md` still says "inline stepper by default"; with Birthday as every event's start, the default a guest meets is the sheet.
- **Enter in the flow:** Enter in a field finishes its step as before, but focus now follows to the new step's first control instead of being left on the hidden field (which in the sheet would also have let it out). Enter on a button now does what the button says: before, Enter on Back or on a plus-one chip moved forward.
- **The name field no longer uses `autoFocus`:** it takes focus as it appears through a ref with `preventScroll`, because a plain focus could scroll the page behind the fixed sheet. Inline, the field is right under the button just tapped, so nothing is lost.
- **The suite hit Better Auth's rate limit, and `e2e/hosts.ts` now says which client each host is.** Better Auth (on in production builds) limits each (client address, path) to 100 requests, and only starts counting again after ten quiet seconds. The verification link is the one limited endpoint the browser opens directly, and its token is in the query, so every host shares one bucket; the test profile has no proxy to tell clients apart, so the suite's hundred-and-first verification was refused (the last eleven tests failed at sign-up). `verifyEmail` now sends a random private `X-Forwarded-For` per host, as the reverse proxy in front of a real instance would.
