# 13: Poster mode

**What to build:** An uploaded image can be the invitation itself: shown at its own proportions as the poster card with a blurred copy behind it, and the title either on the poster with a scrim or below it, at the host's choice.

**Blocked by:** 12 Uploads: storage and processing

**Status:** done

- [x] The Background or Poster choice beside the upload sets the theme's upload mode; Poster renders the image at its natural aspect ratio as the card, with a blurred, darkened copy as the page background.
- [x] A Title placement row appears in the Details group only in poster mode, with On the poster and Below the poster; On the poster adds a gradient scrim so the title stays readable at either text tone.
- [x] A poster-sized rendition is produced on upload for this mode.
- [x] Alternative text applies to the poster image.
- [x] Visual check at 390px and desktop with a portrait and a landscape poster; browser tests cover both placements and switching back to background mode.

## Comments

**2026-09-24, implementing agent.** Done. Lint, type check, 247 unit tests, and 158 browser tests (79 scenarios at 390px and desktop) pass against the production image on the Compose `test` profile. A pixel audit on that image of 952 text runs in poster mode (a portrait and a landscape poster, both text tones, glass and outline buttons, both title placements, 390px and 1280px, top and foot of the page) measured every run at AA against the pixels actually behind it; the closest was 5.64:1.

How it landed:

- **The poster rendition** `poster.webp` is in `RENDITIONS`, so it is stored, served and removed with the others: the picture at its own proportions, fit inside 1200x2400, never enlarged, WebP at quality 85 (a poster's lettering is read close to its pixels). 1200 covers the card, at most 34rem wide, at 2x on a desktop and 3x on a phone. Its size is stored on the upload record (`poster_width`, `poster_height`), so the `<img>` holds its place before it loads.
- **The blurred copy behind the poster** is the same file as a CSS background, blurred 64px (`blur-3xl`) over a box 8rem past every edge of the screen, so the blur's soft edges stay off-screen, under the poster scrim: 45% black in the light tone and 45% white in the dark (`TONES.*.posterScrim`, the prototype's `scrimPoster`). The ticket's "darkened" is the light tone; under dark text the copy is lightened, so text reads on it in either tone. `process.ts` measures it on upload as it measures a background: the picture scaled to cover that box on a 1920x1080 screen, blurred 64px, at a quarter of the size, then `measureBackdrop(raster, "poster")` with the poster scrim. That is stored as `poster_lightest` and `poster_darkest`. In poster mode `resolveTheme` solves every surface on the page against those (`toneTokens(..., "poster")`) and gives the backdrop the poster scrim.
- **Theme:** `ResolvedTheme.poster` (`src`, `width`, `height`, `altText`, `titlePlacement`) is set when the upload is in use and `uploadMode` is `poster`; `upload` is set in either mode, so guests are sent the picture. New `KnobChange`s: `uploadMode` (the event's picture as the background or as the poster: the drawer's "Use it as" row and the gallery's "Your picture" tile send it) and `titlePlacement`. The same picture used the other way keeps the host's accent, so Background, Poster, Background comes back to the same theme; a picture newly in use (from the gallery, or a new upload) puts the accent back on auto, as a new background does. `uploadId` is now the server's alone (a new upload), and keeps the mode the host chose, so replacing a poster keeps it the poster. `parseThemeChange` no longer takes `uploadId` from the browser, and takes `uploadMode` only on an event with an upload. With no id sent by the browser, the race ticket 12 noted (a theme change naming the old upload) is gone.
- **Page:** `PosterCard` (`src/themes/poster-card.tsx`, a client component, as it follows the live theme) is handed its pieces by `PosterLayout`. As before when there is no poster. Below the poster: the poster, rounded and shadowed, then a glass card with the eyebrow, the title, the date sticker and who is hosting. On the poster: the eyebrow and the title at the poster's foot on a frosted scrim (blurred 24px like the glass, and shaded), who is hosting in a glass pill beneath, and no date sticker. A poster taller than the screen is drawn at the screen's height less 6rem, and narrower, but never under 20rem. Beside a poster the title is `compact`: smaller, and sized to the card with container query units rather than to the screen, so it fits a narrow poster. The poster is a `next/image` with `unoptimized` (the rendition is already made to size) and `fetchPriority="high"`.
- **Legibility (`legibility.ts`), the two new surfaces.** Text straight on the blurred copy: every surface of the layout, and the sheet, solved against the copy's measured extremes under the poster scrim. Unit tests cover a black poster through a white one, in both tones, with every accent. The title on the poster: `POSTER_SURFACES`, **solved over anything rather than sampled**. White under light text, black under dark, as the sheet is, because the region the title covers changes with the title's length, the font and the screen's width, so a sampled region would be a guess. It solves to 67% of the light tone's plum and 60% white in the dark tone, with secondary text at 80% on it. The frosting only blends colours already there, so the guarantee holds. Unit tests: 125 colours from black to white under it, both tones, every background; never solid; hierarchy kept. Dropping the "anything" layer fails three of them.
- **Drawer:** "Use it as" offers Background and Poster, with a hint under it in poster mode ("Choose where the title goes under Details"). The Title placement row comes last in Details, after RSVP style, and only in poster mode: On the poster, or Below the poster, with a hint for the one chosen. Changing it marks the template dirty like any knob.
- **Alternative text** is the poster's `<img alt>`. `BackgroundDescription` is not rendered in poster mode, so a screen reader meets the description once (tested).
- **Browser tests**, `e2e/poster.spec.ts`. The poster at its proportions for a guest, described once and on the `<img>`, the page behind it the blurred copy, the title below it, then on it, then below again, and the rendition at the picture's own size. Switching back to the background keeps the same upload, its description and the text tone, and the Title placement row goes. A replaced poster stays the poster, and the old poster's file is gone. `uploads.spec.ts` now expects Poster to be offered.

Decisions and things to know:

- **Migration `0012_poster_uploads` deletes every upload row before adding the columns.** They are NOT NULL, and an upload made before this ticket has no poster rendition, so none can be kept whole. OpenInvites was never released, so only development databases hold any (this machine's held 46; the ones I could see belonged to browser-test hosts). Their events show the default background until the host picks again, and their files are left in the development storage. No fallback or backfill, as ruled. Undo this line if the maintainer would rather reset development databases by hand.
- **The date sticker is left off when the title is on the poster.** A poster usually carries its own date, the When tile has it, and glass over an arbitrary picture would be a surface no rule covers.
- On the poster over the poster's own lettering, that lettering shows frosted under the title, not hidden. A very wide poster (3:1) with a long title on it at 390px is covered by the title's block: the card grows on the base colour rather than lose any of the title. Below the poster is the choice for both, and the host sees it live.
- A poster more than 2.2 times as tall as it is wide is held at 20rem wide, so on a 1280x800 screen its foot runs past the first screen.
- Processing a 48 MP photo now takes about 0.64 s (0.57 s in ticket 12): one more rendition and one more measured raster.

Left for later tickets or noted:

- **Preview card: it plainly misrepresents a portrait poster** (left as it is, per the ruling). `card.jpg` is a 1200x630 cover crop, so a portrait poster unfurls as its middle band under the title. In the visual check, the poster's "SUMMER" headline was gone, "FAIR" was cut in half, and the date line at its foot was lost. A poster-mode card would show the poster whole beside the title, on its blurred copy. That needs a contained rendition in a format Satori reads (JPEG or PNG, not WebP), in `RENDITIONS`.
- **For ticket 20:** new English strings in `DesignDrawer`: `useAsPosterHint`, `titlePlacement`, `titlePlacements.on` and `.below`, `titlePlacementHints.on` and `.below`. On a poster page the poster is the largest paint (`fetchPriority="high"`). The 64px blur of the copy behind it was not measured on a low-end phone, and the poster's alternative text was not checked with a screen reader.
- **For ticket 19:** the uploads volume holds a third file per upload, `<id>-poster.webp`.
